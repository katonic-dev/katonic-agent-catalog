# Meridian Trust Bank (an invented bank, for demonstration only)
# Standard MTB-STD-041: Third-party security and resilience

Version 1.2. Owner: Third-Party Risk Officer. Approved by the Risk Committee, 9 June 2026. Next review: June 2027.

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This standard sets out what the bank requires of a supplier before it uses the supplier's service, and each year after that. It supports policy MTB-POL-033, Technology risk and outsourcing.

1.2 A supplier is tier 1 where its service is critical under MTB-POL-033, clause 2.1, or where it holds or can read customer information. Every other supplier is tier 2.

1.3 Every requirement below applies to a tier 1 supplier. Requirements 2.1, 2.4, 5.1 and 6.1 apply to a tier 2 supplier.

## 2. Information security

2.1 The supplier holds an independent security certification or audit report dated within the last 12 months.

2.2 Customer information is encrypted at rest and in transit.

2.3 Multi-factor authentication is required for every member of the supplier's staff who can reach the bank's information, not only for administrators and not only for remote access.

2.4 The supplier tells the bank of a security incident that affects the bank within 24 hours of detecting it.

2.5 The supplier has a penetration test carried out at least once a year, and closes critical findings within 30 days.

## 3. Data

3.1 Customer information stays in Meridia unless the bank has agreed otherwise in writing. This covers backups.

3.2 The supplier lists its sub-processors and tells the bank at least 30 days before it adds or changes one.

3.3 At the end of the contract the supplier returns or deletes the bank's information within 60 days and gives a certificate.

## 4. Resilience

4.1 The supplier has a business continuity plan that was tested within the last 12 months.

4.2 The recovery time objective for a tier 1 service is no longer than four hours.

4.3 Backups are tested at least twice a year.

## 5. Contract

5.1 The contract gives the bank the right to audit the supplier.

5.2 An exit plan is agreed before the service starts.

5.3 The contract gives the bank the right to be told of sub-contracting.

5.4 A tier 1 supplier holds liability insurance of at least USD 5 million.

## 6. People

6.1 Staff who can reach customer information have passed a background check.

6.2 Staff complete security training at least once a year.

## 7. Assessment and decision

7.1 The supplier answers the bank's questionnaire and gives evidence for each answer. An answer with no evidence, or with evidence that says something else, does not meet the requirement.

7.2 Each requirement is marked met, partly met, not met or not answered, with the reason.

7.3 The Third-Party Risk Officer makes the recommendation: approve, approve with conditions, or do not approve.

7.4 A tier 1 supplier is approved by the Risk Committee, as MTB-POL-033, clause 5.1 requires. A tier 2 supplier is approved by the Head of Procurement.

7.5 A requirement that is not met can be accepted only as a recorded exception approved by the Risk Committee, with an end date.

## 8. What this standard does not cover

8.1 This standard does not set out how the bank assesses a supplier's own use of artificial intelligence in the service it supplies.
